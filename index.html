Decimal AI, Senior Software Engineer

      
Our own price data, in place of the estimating software

      
Door-hardware manufacturers publish their prices as long PDF price books, full of tables, footnotes and add-on charges. I built the pipeline that turns those PDFs into clean price data with Gemini and Claude. It runs in four steps, and a person approves each step before the next one starts.

      
Before a prompt or model change ships, it is scored against a product database we know is correct, so we can see whether accuracy went up or down.

Decimal AI, Senior Software Engineer

      
A project estimate in 10–15 minutes instead of 10+ hours

      
Pricing the door hardware for a project used to mean typing every product code into an estimating tool and picking its options by hand. I worked with estimators on live bids to build an engine that reads the project spec, picks the matching product and options for each line, and prices it from our own price data.

      
Each match gets a confidence score, and the low-confidence ones go to an estimator to decide.

Tankersathi, for my father's water-tanker business

        
Every customer call becomes a one-tap order

        
My father takes every order by phone and used to keep them all in his head. Now when a customer calls, the app recognises the number and shows a one-tap order with their last order filled in. It also tracks what each customer owes, gives drivers their own login, and sends a WhatsApp message when a push notification doesn't arrive.

        
The hard part
Google Play only lets phone and SMS apps read the call log. So the app registers as a caller-ID app, and matches the caller's number on the phone itself without uploading it.

        
0.4sfor the order prompt to appear, down from 25.3s with the usual method

Kramatiq, a trading platform I co-founded. CTO, 2025 to 2026

        
Trade signals delivered to every subscriber in real time

        
Kramatiq sold trading bots that ran inside MetaTrader 5, a desktop trading app. A Node.js server sent each trade signal to every subscriber's bot. Each bot confirmed it, and a missed signal was sent again, or replayed when the bot reconnected.

        
The hard part
MetaTrader's language, MQL5, has no WebSocket support, so a bot couldn't keep a live connection open. I built on an open-source client so the bot used Windows' networking library directly and managed the connection itself.

Kramatiq, selling the bots

        
Bots that only ran for paying subscribers

        
When a bot started, it checked its license key, email and subscription with our API, confirmed it was the latest version, and kept checking in while it ran. One license worked on one terminal at a time. A one-click installer on the website put the bot into the right MetaTrader folder.

        
The hard part
MetaTrader has no way to tie a bot to one paying user, so Kramatiq needed its own licensing.

        
300+monthly paying subscribers, out of 2,000+ registered users
